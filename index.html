The Basics

Event Location

Every Event is hosted at Event Spot unless it's changed for a special event.

Servers

Other servers may be used if both guilds agree; otherwise the match defaults to these.

NA EastVirginia, USA
NA WestCalifornia, USA
NA East vs WestIllinois, USA
EuropeNorth Holland, NL

War Sizes

  • Default size is 4v4.
  • Wars can be 3v3 or 5v5 if both guilds agree.
  • With no agreement, the match defaults to 4v4.

Player Cosmetic Requirements

  • Each player must have the guild tag of the guild they are registered under in-game during matches.
  • You can not have any hair IDs that hide or overly obstruct your avatar, or hand other players hair IDs that break this rule via Fade Trimmer.

General Knowledge

  • During a match you only need to listen to the host. If someone says wait, you don't have to — keep fighting.
  • If you're not ready, call it at the start of the match.

Knock Rule

If you are knocked, you are out. You may not get back up and rejoin the fight. (Does not apply in FFA's)

Redos — Overview

Redos are only called under the conditions in Redo Rules. Once a guild calls one, the host's unbiased judgment (mods may chime in) decides its legitimacy by vote. Only then does a redo happen — and once it's played out, the decision can't be overturned.

Depths Wagers

  1. All agreements to unban or change wager conditions must be written in the wager ticket, not said in voice chat.
  2. You must have Bell before wagering.
  3. Only one redo can be called per wager.

1v1 Event Rules

In a 1v1 event, all mantras, weapons, oaths and enchants are unbanned. There are no set bans — the two players agree on what to ban for the fight.

Still Off-Limits

Techs, move stacks, bugs and misc bans (campfires, etc.) stay banned during every event.

Excessive Running

Excessive running is a DQ. You may only run to help teammates.

Making The Call

  • Only the host can call excessive running. Players and stage members can't push the host into making the call.
  • It has to be blatant — back turned, actively running away.

What Counts

  • Over 10 seconds of it.
  • The runner hasn't had a close-range attack blocked, parried or dodged.
  • The runner hasn't been hit multiple times (2+ attacks).

FFA Running

In FFA's, running is allowed — as long as it's not to hide. Treat an FFA like getting ganked in the Depths on a public server: if you're clearly not winning, run. Once the host sees the player count drop low enough, running is re-banned.

Two Strike Rules

Strikes reset every round.

  • Proccing punishment off teammates
  • Wall climbing
  • Roll M1s, crits, uppercuts [Allowed with Gale Dash]

Redo Rules

Redos are only called under specific conditions. Once a redo has played out, the result is final.

Every Redo Needs A Clip

  • You have 5 minutes after the match ends to make a specific redo call and show the clip. The call has to come from someone on the roster.
  • Once you've called a redo for a particular rule break, you can't change the call.

Miss either of those and there's no redo.

Calling A Redo

  1. Call it mid-match — notify the other team and the host.
  2. Screen-record / clip whatever caused it. (Not required if you're being ganked)

Miss either step and no redo is given. A fraudulent call can cost your team the round, at host discretion.

Can't Chat?

If your chat is off and you can't call it out, run from the fight and make obvious, odd movements to signal something's wrong. The host will reach out and pause the fight to sort it.

No Call, No Redo

If the round finishes and no rule break was called out during it, the result stands — there's no redo after the fact. Flag it mid-match or it's final.

Redo CAN Be Called

  • Game itself crashes
  • Excessive running over 12 seconds [Painfully obvious; 50/50s default to no call]
  • An opponent is caught using a banned item / tech / misc
  • The match is interrupted by gankers

Redo CANNOT Be Called

  • Getting sent into the air and taking damage [Depends on damage taken and drop distance]
  • Both teams broke a rule — neither side gets one
  • Asking a different host or moderator for a second opinion [Automatic DQ unless an owner overrides it]
  • Postponing a redo without both guilds agreeing [The guild ready to fight wins by forfeit]

How A Valid Redo Plays Out

  • The round does not fully restart.
  • It resumes with however many players were alive when the redo was called.
  • Every surviving player starts at 100% health.

Example: a 2v4 war gets interrupted by gankers. It restarts as a 2v4 at full HP — not a clean reset.
